On a Spring Day of Passing Whispers

The purpose of this workshop is to help architecture studio students structure their design intentions as a visual language and learn how to communicate them clearly to others. In particular, drawing and rendering are treated not as simple outputs, but as a process of translating and organizing one's thoughts outward. This is to narrow the gap between design intention and interpretation. This gap appears when design thinking and expression operate separately. Expression that does not deliver the core logic remains in form¹. Therefore, this workshop repositions expression as a process that connects intention and meaning.

The workshop proceeds in the following order: providing pre-session materials, demonstrating the working process, hands-on practice, and feedback. The tutor demonstrates the working process — from modeling to drawings to rendering — based on the pre-session materials, and presents to participants the flow of the production process and the criteria for judgment. Participants then transform the drawings and images assigned to them into images that hint at a specific event, and deliver one sentence that represents that event. For example, "Something I valued has disappeared," or "Water is leaking in the passage." They may also arrange or remove objects related to their personal interests or identity. This is set up as a device for inserting one's own story into the work, and to form a structure that checks how the intended story reads to others. Before the session, the tutor provides pre-session materials to guide the work within a limited time. After the session, a communication channel is kept open to respond to individual feedback requests.

The story is set around what happens in a temporary residence where 14 architecture studio students are staying. The structure unfolds with each participant presenting one image and one sentence in parallel. Different perspectives and interpretations accumulate to form a single narrative.

¹ This refers to the state of remaining in existence. An arbitrary object drifts through the void while carrying direction and intention yet to be interpreted. Meanwhile, the moment of interpreting the direction and intention of existence is the moment of blooming of essence and meaning. Herein, blooming is the starting point of a simultaneous and continuous phenomenon of discovery and creation — the process that follows is becoming. Simply put, a letter in a mailbox comes out, is read, and responses and creation continue.
